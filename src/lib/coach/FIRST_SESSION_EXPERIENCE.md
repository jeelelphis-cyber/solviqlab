# First Session Experience v1
# SolviqLAB | CEO Document | Product Design
# Date: 2026-07-24

> "The first two minutes are not an introduction to the product.
> They are the beginning of a relationship."

---

## The One Question This Document Answers

**At what exact second does the user decide to come back tomorrow?**

Everything else is secondary.

---

## The Emotional Arc

```
Fear / Scepticism
        ↓
Curiosity
        ↓
Recognition ("she knows me")
        ↓
Surprise ("I didn't know this about myself")
        ↓
Anticipation ("what's coming next?")
        ↓
Investment ("I want to find out")
```

We are not selling a subscription.
We are moving a person through this arc in 120 seconds.

---

## Second-by-Second Map

### 00:00 — First contact
**What the user sees:** Assessment score + breakdown
**What the user feels:** "Did I do well? What does this number mean?"
**Our job:** Make the score feel personal, not generic.

The score is not a grade. It is a mirror.
> Wrong: "Your health score is 62/100"
> Right: "You scored 62. That's data — not a judgement."

---

### 00:15 — The system starts moving
**What the user sees:** Below the results, something new appears — quietly
**What the user feels:** "Wait, there's more?"
**Our job:** The transition from Assessment results → Mia section must feel like a door opening, not a page reload.

No "MEET YOUR AI COACH" banner.
No confetti.
Just: a face, a name, a calm sentence.

> "Based on your results, I'm preparing something for you."

---

### 00:30 — The name moment
**What the user sees:** Mia asks for their first name
**What the user feels:** "She wants to know who I am"
**Our job:** This must NOT feel like a form field.

One question. One field. No progress bar.
Copy matters:
> Wrong: "Please enter your name to continue"
> Right: "What should I call you?"

The goal selector that follows is not a menu.
It is Mia starting to understand.

---

### 00:50 — The signal
**What the user sees:** "Mia is reviewing your results, {name}…"
**What the user feels:** "She's actually working on this. For me. Right now."
**Our job:** This is the most important 40 seconds in the product.

The loading screen is not a loading screen.
It is proof that something real is happening.

Three requirements:
1. Uses their name — minimum twice
2. Shows what Mia is specifically doing — not generic "loading"
3. Feels like a person working, not a spinner

Example copy sequence (cycles every 8 seconds):
- "Reviewing your {cluster} data, {name}…"
- "Looking at your score — 62. I want to show you what that number actually means for you."
- "Finding the one thing in your profile that matters most right now."
- "Almost done. I want to get this right."

---

### 01:20 — The video begins
**What the user sees:** Mia's face. She says their name.
**What the user feels:** "She knows my name. She's talking to me."
**Our job:** The first 3 seconds of video are everything.

Mia does NOT start with:
- "Hi!"
- "Welcome!"
- "Great to meet you!"

She starts with the name and the number.
> "Hey {name}. Your score is 62. That's real data — not a guess."

The first sentence establishes:
- She knows who they are
- She has their data
- She is direct

---

### 01:28 — The unexpected insight
**What the user sees:** Mia is explaining something they didn't know
**What the user feels:** "How did she know that? That's exactly my situation."
**Our job:** The insight must NOT be something they could have Googled.

> Wrong: "You should sleep more and eat less sugar."
> Right: "Your metabolism score is actually working against your sleep quality. Most people don't see this connection."

The user should feel slightly surprised.
Not overwhelmed. Surprised.

---

### 01:45 — The hook
**What the user sees:** Mia is wrapping up. She promises something.
**What the user feels:** "I want to know what she's preparing."
**Our job:** Create genuine curiosity about tomorrow.

The hook is not a CTA.
The hook is a promise made by a person who takes it seriously.

> "I'm already looking at something in your data that most people miss.
> Come back tomorrow and I'll show you."

After this sentence, the user should feel slightly restless.
Like they've been told the beginning of a story, not the end.

---

### 02:00 — Premium appears
**What the user sees:** "Ready for your full plan, {name}?"
**What the user feels:** "I want to continue this conversation."
**Our job:** The Premium CTA must feel like the natural next step in a conversation — not a paywall.

Three elements:
1. Mia's voice in the copy (not corporate)
2. What they're getting — not features, but what happens next
3. Low-friction entry — 7 days free, no credit card required to start

> Wrong: "Upgrade to Premium — $19.99/month"
> Right: "Mia is already preparing your first weekly plan.
>         7 days free. No card needed.
>         Start when you're ready."

---

## The Four Moments That Decide Everything

| Second | Moment | If we get this wrong |
|--------|--------|---------------------|
| 00:30 | Name ask | Feels like a form → user clicks away |
| 00:50 | Loading screen | Feels generic → trust is lost |
| 01:20 | First video sentence | Says "Hi!" → disappointment |
| 01:45 | Hook | Too vague → no anticipation |

---

## What "Polish" Actually Means

Polish is not visual design.
Polish is the difference between:

> "Loading your video…"

and

> "Mia is reviewing your 62-point score right now, {name}.
> She wants to show you one specific thing."

Every word is a choice.
Every choice either builds or breaks trust.

---

## Launch Criterion (from CEO Review №001)

> "After this video, I myself would want to come back tomorrow."

This document is the brief for achieving that criterion.
